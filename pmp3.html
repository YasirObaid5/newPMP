PMBOK 7: The Value Framework

The 12 Principles (The "Why")

These are the guiding behaviors and mindsets for *all* project management.

  • Be a diligent, respectful, and caring steward
  • Create a collaborative project team environment
  • Effectively engage with stakeholders
  • Focus on value
  • Recognize, evaluate, and respond to system interactions
  • Demonstrate leadership behaviors
  • Tailor based on context
  • Build quality into processes and deliverables
  • Navigate complexity
  • Optimize risk responses
  • Embrace adaptability and resiliency
  • Enable change to achieve the envisioned future state

The 8 Performance Domains (The "What")

These are the broad areas of activity that drive value delivery.

1. Stakeholders

Working with stakeholders to maintain alignment and foster positive relationships.

2. Team

Building a culture of accountability and respect that enables high performance.

3. Development Approach & Life Cycle

Selecting the right approach (predictive, adaptive, hybrid) and delivery cadence.

4. Planning

Organizing and coordinating the work needed to deliver the project's outcomes.

5. Project Work

Managing project processes, resources, and learning to get the work done efficiently.

6. Delivery

Delivering the project's scope and quality to meet objectives and realize value.

7. Measurement

Assessing project performance and taking action to maintain acceptable progress.

8. Uncertainty

Navigating risk, ambiguity, and complexity in the project.

Agile: The Adaptive Framework

over processes and tools

over comprehensive documentation

over contract negotiation

Responding to change

over following a plan

The 12 Agile Principles

  1. Our highest priority is to satisfy the customer through early and continuous delivery of valuable software.
  2. Welcome changing requirements, even late in development.
  3. Deliver working software frequently, from a couple of weeks to a couple of months.
  4. Business people and developers must work together daily.
  5. Build projects around motivated individuals. Give them the environment and support they need.
  6. The most efficient and effective method of conveying information is face-to-face conversation.
  7. Working software is the primary measure of progress.
  8. Agile processes promote sustainable development.
  9. Continuous attention to technical excellence and good design enhances agility.
  10. Simplicity—the art of maximizing the amount of work not done—is essential.
  11. The best architectures, requirements, and designs emerge from self-organizing teams.
  12. At regular intervals, the team reflects on how to become more effective, then tunes and adjusts its behavior.

Common Practices

Scrum (Iterative)

A framework for working in fixed-length iterations called **Sprints** to deliver a potentially shippable increment of product.

  • **Roles:** Product Owner, Scrum Master, Developers
  • **Events:** Sprint, Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum, Sprint Review, Sprint Retrospective
  • **Artifacts:** Product Backlog, Sprint Backlog, Product Increment
  • **Best for:** Complex projects with changing requirements where incremental delivery adds value.

Kanban (Flow-Based)

A method for visualizing and managing workflow. The goal is to optimize the flow of value from "To Do" to "Done."

  • **Core Principles:** Visualize the workflow, Limit Work in Progress (WIP), Manage flow, Make policies explicit
  • **Key Feature:** A "Kanban Board" with columns representing stages of work.
  • **Metrics:** Cycle Time, Throughput
  • **Best for:** Work with a continuous flow, like support teams, ops, or projects where priorities change daily.
